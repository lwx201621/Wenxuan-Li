Wenxuan Li.

During my undergraduate studies, I have been mentored by Prof. Xiaomin Li and Prof. Beijia Tan. My research focuses on children’s nature connectedness, psychological resilience, and family relationships. Through micro-level longitudinal research, I examine both differences between children and changes within the same child over time, seeking to understand how they grow within their everyday environments.

Studying these micro-level processes has also led me to recognize that children’s immediate environments are not isolated from wider educational and social conditions. I therefore extend my work to cross-national comparisons, examining developmental patterns and opportunities for support across contexts, and connecting individual children’s experiences with a broader educational picture.

About

🧑‍🔬 Researcher, still becoming.

I want to understand how children grow within their everyday environments—and what their experiences look like when we step back to see the wider picture.

What draws me to child development is that growth is never separate from context. Children’s relationships, attitudes, and resilience develop within everyday experiences at home, in school, and in nature. I want to understand how these pieces fit together, rather than treating each measure as an isolated outcome.

Longitudinal data let me stay with a question beyond a single snapshot. They help me distinguish relatively stable differences between children from changes within the same child, and ask how experiences and psychological resources develop alongside one another.

Cross-national research widens that view. Using large-scale public surveys, I explore how learning, teaching, and early developmental support vary across settings, and how socioeconomic differences are linked to access to support. I look for shared patterns without losing the contexts that make countries and education systems different.

I am most interested in where these perspectives meet: when an individual developmental pattern raises a broader question about opportunity, or a cross-country comparison brings me back to the experiences behind an average. I want to use quantitative tools to ask clearer questions while keeping the people represented by the data in view.

Representative work

🔬 Nature as a developmental resource.

A closer look at a longitudinal study of nature connectedness and early childhood development.

01 🌱 Child development · Longitudinal

How does nature connectedness develop alongside resilience and family relationships?

This project treats nature connectedness as a developmental environmental resource rather than a fixed preference. Using longitudinal growth models, it examines how children’s connection with nature changes alongside psychological resilience and parent–child conflict. By separating initial levels from rates of change, the analysis considers both children’s starting points and the pace of their development within psychological and family contexts. The focus is not only on whether these factors are related at one moment, but on how their trajectories unfold together over time.
